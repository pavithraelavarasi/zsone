Orientation Walkthrough

See a webpage before you build one.

A webpage is not magic and it is not just design on a screen. It is a set of instructions a browser reads. HTML is the structure, CSS is the styling, and the visible page is the result those two produce.

Most beginners rush into typing code before they can recognize layout, hierarchy, and behavior. This walkthrough slows that down on purpose. You are not building yet. You are learning to look — with a live playground in every part.

Part 01

Anatomy of a page

What you see in a browser is the result. Underneath sit two kinds of decisions: the HTML that says what things are and how they nest, and the CSS that says how they should look. Change the HTML and you change the structure. Type below and watch the page rebuild in real time.

Part 02

HTML — the structure

HTML is like the framing and rooms of a house: it decides what exists and how it is arranged, before anything is painted. Almost every page starts from the same small skeleton. Assemble it below, one part at a time.

Playground · Build the skeleton
<!DOCTYPE html>+ <html>+ <head>+ <body> 
Press “Build the page” to assemble the skeleton.
Four parts, in order, make a complete page.
The takeaway. That skeleton is not trivia. <head> holds information about the page, <body> holds what you actually see, and the whole thing is the starting point for nearly every page you will build.

          Part 05
          
Visual hierarchy

          

            Why is the biggest text big and the smallest text small? Size,
            weight, and color tell your eye what matters most and what to read
            first. This is the same instinct behind the orientation questions:
            what grabbed you first, and where did your eye move next. Adjust the
            controls and feel how the order of attention shifts.

          Part 06
          
Patterns and templates

            repeated buttons, a row of feature boxes. Same layout, different
            content. That repetition is usually one template filled by a list of
            data. Change how many cards you want and watch a single pattern

          Part 10
          
Understanding

          

            Three ideas hold the whole picture together. HTML gives a page its
            structure. CSS changes how that structure looks. The browser is the
            tool that reads both and draws the result. Once you can see those
            layers in any page you visit, you are ready to start writing them
            yourself.
          

          

            

              HTML — what things are and how they nest. The framing of
              the house.
            

            

              CSS — how those things look. The paint, spacing, and
              finish.
            

          

          

            The browser reads both and produces the page you see.
            Right-click and Inspect any site to watch the connection live.
